- `commands.touchDrag` and `commands.touchStep` resolve once the page has handled the touch event they sent, and throw after 5 seconds if it has not, naming the event and the touch events the page did handle. Before, a test that read its touch listeners as soon as a swipe returned could miss the touchend or a touchmove: once the page scrolls, Chromium hands it the rest of the gesture without waiting. A `touchStep` gesture that scrolls is slower for it: each `touchMove` step after the scroll starts takes up to about 200ms, the rate at which Chromium hands a scrolling page its touchmoves, so the finger moves slower and a test that depends on its speed at lift may change. `touchDrag` waits once, after the touchend, and its speed is unchanged. While a gesture runs, both commands keep passive touch listeners on the window of each frame.
