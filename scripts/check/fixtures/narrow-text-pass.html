Narrow text

Prose with no cap runs the full width of its column, so every line fills the space the page gives it, as a reader of a wide screen expects.

A centred lede is not a hard wrap, so a short cap on it is a choice of layout rather than a reading measure that holds the text back.

A sidebar is narrow by its layout, not by a cap on its words, so its text wraps at the width the sidebar was given and passes here.

The body beside the sidebar takes the width left over, with no cap on its words, so the column fills the rest of the row.

A card holds its own text to its own width by design, as a card should, so a narrow card is not a reading measure in a wide page.

A table cell is narrow by its column, and its words wrap where the column ends, which is how a table is meant to read.
